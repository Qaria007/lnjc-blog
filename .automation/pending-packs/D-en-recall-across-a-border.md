## Why this topic

`ar/product-recall-and-withdrawal.html` and `ar/complaints-handling.html` cover this from the
distributor's side in Arabic. The English side has nothing. A manufacturer deciding whether to
appoint a distributor in a market it cannot easily reach is entitled to ask what happens on the
worst day, and the answer is one of the sharper tests of whether a partner is real.

There is also a live accuracy hazard on this exact topic, which is the reason to write it
carefully rather than avoid it. See below.

## IMPORTANT, READ BEFORE WRITING

The recall trap. `.automation/README.md` records that a draft written from search snippets claimed
the EU Good Distribution Practice guidelines require a periodic dummy or mock recall when no real
recall has happened. They do not. The words dummy and mock do not appear in them. Consultancy
pages repeat this claim to each other and it is wrong.

So: state what the guidelines say about testing the effectiveness of a recall procedure only if the
verbatim text you extract actually says it, and quote it as it stands. If you find yourself
reaching for a frequency, a periodicity, or the word mock, stop and reread the source. If the
source does not support it, the sentence does not go in. Given the history, this article should
probably say plainly that the requirement is often misquoted, and what the text actually says.

The usual rule also applies. Aimed at manufacturers outside Yemen. No Yemen specific regulatory
requirements, no registration timelines, no document lists. Explain the international framework,
then be plain that local specifics are confirmed case by case, and invite the reader to ask.
