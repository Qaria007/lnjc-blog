IMPORTANT, READ BEFORE WRITING. This article is aimed at manufacturers outside Yemen who are
looking for a distribution partner. You will be tempted to state Yemen specific regulatory
requirements: how long SBDMA registration takes, exactly which documents it wants, what it
charges. DO NOT. The Yemeni authority's own website is unreachable, and almost everything
published on this subject online comes from consultancies selling registration services, who
contradict each other. Nothing in this pack is a Yemen specific regulatory requirement and you
may not present anything as one.

Specific to this topic: neither document below mentions the phrase "freight forwarder" in a
qualification checklist. The EU guidelines speak of the "contract giver" and "contract acceptor"
for any outsourced activity and say the transport contract should encompass those requirements.
The WHO guidance speaks of carriers, common carriers and contractors under a service level
agreement, and mentions a freight forwarder only once, as one party that can carry out a
customs pre-clearance procedure. Write the article as the application of those outsourcing and
transport principles to a forwarder, and say that this is the reading, not a quotation. Do not
invent audit checklists, certifications (for example any named accreditation), numeric
thresholds, audit frequencies other than "risk based", or a list of forwarders. Do not claim that
either document names the forwarder as a regulated party. The WHO text carries odd word breaks
from PDF conversion (for example "carr ier", "proﬁ le"); copy them exactly if quoting directly.
The WHO abbreviation TTSPP means time- and temperature-sensitive pharmaceutical product; the
EU excerpts cover "medicinal products". Do not state what Yemen requires of any forwarder.

7.1. Principle
Any activity covered by the GDP guide that is outsourced
should be correctly defined, agreed and controlled in order to
avoid misunderstandings which could affect the integrity of the
product. There must be a written contract between the contract
giver and the contract acceptor which clearly establishes the
duties of each party.
7.2. Contract giver
The contract giver is responsible for the activities contracted
out.
The contract giver is responsible for assessing the competence
of the contract acceptor to successfully carry out the work
required and for ensuring by means of the contract and
through audits that the principles and guidelines of GDP are
followed. An audit of the contract acceptor should be
performed before commencement of, and whenever there has
been a change to, the outsourced activities. The frequency of
audit should be defined based on risk depending on the nature
of the outsourced activities. Audits should be permitted at any
time.
The contract giver should provide the contract acceptor with all
the information necessary to carry out the contracted operations
in accordance with the specific product requirements and any
other relevant requirements.
7.3. Contract acceptor
The contract acceptor should have adequate premises and
equipment, procedures, knowledge and experience, and
competent personnel to carry out the work ordered by the
contract giver.

The contract acceptor should refrain from any activity which
may adversely affect the quality of the product(s) handled for
the contract giver.
The contract acceptor must forward any information that can
influence the quality of the product(s) to the contract giver in
accordance with the requirement of the contract.
