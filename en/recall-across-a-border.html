Recalling an exported pharmaceutical batch

By LNJC· 1 October 2026· 6 min read

When a batch has already been exported and must be recalled, the European Commission's Good Distribution Practice guidelines say the distributor must follow the recall message, and distribution records must cover exported product. WHO guidance adds that customers and authorities in every country supplied are informed promptly, ending in a final report.

This article is for manufacturers and exporters who sell to a distributor abroad and want to know what a recall looks like once product has crossed a border. It explains the international framework. It does not describe any Yemen specific rule, and it does not say when a recall is justified for a given product. That is a quality decision for the manufacturer.

What do the guidelines say must happen in a recall?

They say recall must be possible at any time, and that the distributor acts on the recall message. The European Commission's Good Distribution Practice guidelines of 5 November 2013 (2013/C 343/01), section 6.5, state that recall operations should be capable of being initiated promptly and at any time. The distributor must follow the instructions of a recall message, which should be approved, if required, by the competent authorities.1

Section 6.5 also says any recall operation should be recorded at the time it is carried out, with records made readily available to the competent authorities, and that the progress of the recall should be recorded for a final report.1

Who has to be told when the product has been exported?

Everyone downstream, in every country the product reached. WHO Technical Report Series No. 1025, Annex 7 (2020), Good storage and distribution practices for medical products, section 10, says the original manufacturer or marketing authorization holder should be informed in the event of a recall, and that information on the recall should be shared with the appropriate national or regional regulatory authority. It adds that all customers and competent authorities of all countries to which a given medical product may have been distributed should be informed promptly.2

For temperature-sensitive products, WHO Technical Report Series No. 961, Annex 9 (2011) is more explicit about borders. Its recall procedures list notifying the local regulatory authority, notifying overseas regulatory counterparts where the product has been exported, and notifying all affected customers as applicable.3

What records make a recall possible?

Distribution records that identify every customer and batch, including exported product. The EU guidelines say the records should be readily accessible to the person or persons responsible for the recall. They should contain sufficient information on distributors and directly supplied customers: addresses, phone or fax numbers inside and outside working hours, batch numbers and quantities delivered, including those for exported products and samples.1

WHO Technical Report Series No. 1025, Annex 7, section 10.7 asks for the same kind of detail: name, address, contact detail, batch numbers, quantities and safety features, including exported products.2 The practical point for an exporter is that your distributor can only recall what it can trace, so the records are worth checking before the first shipment rather than during a recall.

What happens to recalled product?

It is secured, segregated and clearly labelled. WHO Technical Report Series No. 1025, Annex 7, section 10.5 says all recalled products should be secure, segregated, transported and stored under appropriate conditions, clearly labelled as recalled, with the particular storage conditions applicable to the product maintained where possible.2

For time and temperature sensitive products, WHO Technical Report Series No. 961, Annex 9 says recalled product should be marked for disposal as either "recalled" or "withdrawn", and transported back from the recipient and quarantined under secure conditions pending a final decision on disposal. Remaining inventory is quarantined and marked for further investigation before disposal.3

How does a recall end?

With a final report that accounts for the quantities. WHO Technical Report Series No. 1025, Annex 7, section 10.8 says the progress of a recall should be recorded and a final report issued, including a reconciliation between delivered and recovered quantities.2 WHO Technical Report Series No. 961, Annex 9 likewise asks for records of all recalls, including reconciliation of quantity sold, returned, remaining or consumed.3

Where a recall concerns falsified product, WHO Technical Report Series No. 1019, Annex 5 (2019), section 7.7 says national regulatory authorities should notify other national or regional authorities and the WHO Global Surveillance and Monitoring System of confirmed cases of imported substandard or falsified products, without delay.4

Does Good Distribution Practice require a mock recall every year?

No, although this is often misquoted online. Section 6.5 of the EU guidelines says the effectiveness of the arrangements for product recall should be evaluated regularly, at least annually.1 WHO Technical Report Series No. 1025, Annex 7, section 10.2 says the effectiveness of the procedure should be checked annually and updated as necessary.2

Neither text says a test recall of real product must be run. They require the arrangements to be evaluated, and leave the method to the distributor. When you assess a partner, ask how it carries out that annual check and what it records.

What should you agree with a distributor before shipping?

Agree who issues a recall message, who is told, and how quickly records can be produced. This is common industry practice, not a requirement drawn from a named document. Ask to see the distributor's written recall procedure, and check that it names the person responsible and covers customers in other countries, since WHO section 10.1 expects a written procedure in compliance with national or regional requirements.2

Frequently asked questions

What happens when a pharmaceutical batch is recalled after export?

The distributor follows the recall message, which should be approved by the competent authorities if required. WHO Technical Report Series No. 1025, Annex 7 says customers and authorities in every country supplied are informed promptly, recalled product is secured and segregated, and a final report reconciles delivered and recovered quantities.

Do EU Good Distribution Practice guidelines require a mock recall every year?

No. Section 6.5 of the European Commission's guidelines (2013/C 343/01) says the effectiveness of the arrangements for product recall should be evaluated regularly, at least annually. It does not say a test recall of real product must be run. How the evaluation is done is left to the distributor.

What distribution records are needed to run a recall on exported product?

The EU guidelines say distribution records should give sufficient information on directly supplied customers, including addresses, phone numbers, batch numbers and quantities delivered, and include exported products. WHO Technical Report Series No. 1025, Annex 7 asks for the same detail, and that the records are readily accessible to the person responsible for recalls.
